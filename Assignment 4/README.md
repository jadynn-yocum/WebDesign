# Assignment 4 - README Questions

### Visual Appeal
* **Which icons did you ultimately use and for what purposes?**
  * I went with clean, minimalist line icons from Lucide. I added 'home', 'user', and 'info' icons right into my main navigation links so the menu feels intuitive. Down in the contact section, I used social and utility icons (like the Instagram camera, GitHub logo, and a mail envelope) to make my personal links pop and look professional.
* **Which consistent colors, values, lines, and/or shapes did you utilize and why?**
  * Since my portfolio focuses on outdoor recreation and public lands, I used a rich forest green (`#116446`) as my main anchor color. I paired it with soft, paper-like off-white boxes (`#f7f6f2`) for my cards and containers. I kept my borders thin and used crisp dividers to give the site an editorial, magazine-style feel that doesn't distract from my photography.
* **Which Google Fonts did you use and for what purposes?**
  * I chose 'Playfair Display' for all my big headers because it has a classic, high-end editorial look. For the main body text, I paired it with 'Inter'—it's super clean, easy to read on phone screens, and keeps the design feeling modern.

### Structure & Organization
* **What information does your table organize, and why was a table appropriate?**
  * My table lays out the freelance creative services I offer, breaking down my deliverables and estimated turnaround times. A table was the perfect choice here because trying to write all of that out in paragraphs would look messy. This way, potential clients can scan my packages in seconds.
* **What specific style choices helped you structure and organize your form?**
  * I built the contact form with a clean, stacked layout so it's super easy to fill out. I used generous padding inside the inputs, helpful placeholder text, and added a green focus border so users always know exactly which field they are currently typing in.

### Design Goals
* **What is the goal of your form, and how did it support your site's goals?**
  * The form gives people a direct way to get in touch with me. Whether someone wants to book a photoshoot, collaborate on a marketing project, or ask about design assets, it turns my portfolio into an active business tool instead of just a static gallery.
* **How does the gallery support your website's goals?**
  * As a photographer and visual designer, proof is everything. The gallery grid cleanly showcases my best landscape work and publication layouts, proving to clients that I have the technical skills to back up my services.
* **What pattern did you use: uniform, alternating, or something else, and why?**
  * I stuck with a uniform multi-column card grid. Because my photos have different colors and textures, keeping the structural layout exactly the same helps the whole site feel cohesive, balanced, and intentional.

### Checking In
* **Learning about and applying design principles:** It's really cool seeing how small tweaks to white space and alignment completely change how professional a page looks.
* **Using typography in your designs:** Finding a good font pair was a game-changer. Moving away from system fonts immediately made my site feel like a real publication.
* **Creating components (like navs, cards, galleries, forms, buttons, and tables):** Building these out as separate pieces makes organizing a website layout feel like playing with blocks—it just clicks.
* **Exploring display: flex:** Switching from old-school positioning to Flexbox made alignment so much easier. It's awesome not having to fight my elements to make them sit next to each other.
